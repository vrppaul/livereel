# Changelog

## 0.3.0

- `camera.onto`, `light.on`, `light.to` and `cursor.to` refuse, as they begin, a block standing wholly off the page's screen, saying where it stands and how to bring it in: above or below, with `page.scrollTo` ending before the move begins; beside the screen, with an act (`cursor.to's [data-testid=ask] stands off the page's screen, at left 8, top -3097 of a screen 480 × 320: …`). They refuse a block the page lays out at no size, hidden or holding others alone, in its own words. The camera had stopped at the screen's nearest edge, the light lit nothing and the cursor left the screen, without a word. A block partly on the screen is still framed. The skill no longer lists it as a trap.
- Breaking: on the stage apart from the app, where a film names none, the page is framed as it comes, nothing of its answers touched. A page that refuses to be framed is refused, its refusal named (`the page at … refuses to be framed (X-Frame-Options: DENY): film it with stage: "inside", …`), as the frame opens it, after the act that led the frame to it, before the next frame, or in place of whatever a step then failed at on Chrome's error page (`nothing on the page matches h1`), where livereel had taken the refusal off; `examples/example-com.ts`, whose link leads to IANA's page, which refuses, stands its stage inside example.com. A page reached through a redirect lands at its own address, its relative addresses whole, where it had kept the address asked for. A click that takes the stage away, as a link to `_top` does, is refused, saying so.
- Chrome's Local Network Access checks are no longer turned off: the filming browser is granted `local-network-access` instead, for every page. A page the stage inside the app answers anew hears nothing its server on this machine pushes down a WebSocket without it.
- A film can be shot on the stage inside its app: `stage: 'inside'` in `defineFilm` stands the stage on the app's own address, at `/__livereel__/stage.html`, so the page in its frame is of the stage's own site. An app that signs in is filmed signed in: its cookies go with each of its requests, and a sign-in the film makes in the frame holds. A redirect lands in the frame, and a page that refuses to be framed is shown, the refusal taken off that page's answer alone. The frame is sandboxed short of moving the page it stands in, so a page that breaks out of frames stays in the stage. `stage: 'apart'` is where none is named.
- `--renderer gpu` that reaches no graphics card warns of it on stderr, as Chrome tells it ("--renderer gpu reached no graphics card (Chrome's compositing is disabled_software)"), and shoots the film in software all the same: it had drawn in software without a word. What 0.2.0 said of WebGL then was wrong: on Linux a page keeps its WebGL where Vulkan finds no driver at all, through SwiftShader, and has none where Vulkan finds only Mesa's software lavapipe (measured 2026-10-27).
- What a film hands `defineFilm` and every function it calls is checked as the film is defined, and its steps and what its `serve` gives as it is served: a value of the wrong kind, a name the function does not take, or one it needs left out is refused where it stands, down to the item or the name at fault (`camera.onto's air is "80": it is a number`, `page.type's strokes[2]'s typed is undefined`). It had been read as given: `cursor.click(action, { sound: null })` failed at the shoot on Node's own error, `formats: null` was said as a film that is no ES module, and `frame: {}` reached the stage as a zoom of NaN. An endless number stays a number where it means no bound (`zoomAtMost: Infinity`).
- A frame whose sides are not whole, even counts of pixels is refused as the film is defined, for a still as for a video: `still` shot a 321 × 320 frame. A screen's sides are more than none.
- The steps of a `cursor.click` or a `typing` left in a list of their own, not spread among the film's steps, are refused with how to spread them; a step written out by hand is held to its kind's form.
- A sound or music file named by an empty path is refused as the film is defined, as `sound.play('')` is.
- The hint that a film is an ES module is given only where the film does not parse, not with whatever a film throws as it is run.

## 0.2.1

- A film's `sounds`, `motionBlur` or `music` written in none of their forms is refused as the film is defined, where it stands and as it was written (`the film's motionBlur.shutter is null: …`): `null` or a list in place of an object, a name the form has not (`enable` for `enabled`), `enabled` neither `true` nor `false`, a value of the wrong kind (`keyboard: null`). Such a `null` or a misspelt `enable: false` had left livereel's sounds and blur on, and music written as a path alone was refused for its volume.
- The README and the skill say what they left out: `cursor.click` and `typing` give several steps, spread among the others (`...cursor.click(action)`); every command and option of the command line, in a table; that livereel runs a film with its types unchecked; how long a full shoot takes; that `--renderer gpu` draws in software without a word where it finds no driver. The README names every function a film can call, as the skill does, and the gate that held the skill holds both.

## 0.2.0

- Breaking: a film names its formats, each a frame and the screen its page is laid out on, and is shot in one at a time: `formats: { square: …, wide: … }` in place of `format`, so a film of 0.1 gives its one format a name. `--format` names the format to shoot, and a film of one format needs none; `steps` is handed the format's name after the whole screen's pose, for a pose given in numbers. Every file is named after the film file and the format: `film-square.mp4`, `film-square-sheet.png`, `film-square-6.5s.jpg`.
- Breaking: `--quality draft|finest` names how finely a film or a still is shot, in place of `--draft`, which is refused now: `finest`, the default, as before without it, and `draft` as `--draft` was. A name it does not take is refused, naming those it does.
- `livereel shoot --as webm` and `--as gif` make the video as a WebM (VP9) or a GIF, each at the film's own frame and rate, from the one take; `--as mp4 --as gif` gives both. A kind the system's ffmpeg cannot make is refused before the film is shot.
- A film is heard: a click as the cursor presses, a key for every stroke typed into the page or the terminal, a chime as the ending comes up, each laid on the frame it is made on. livereel's own sounds are CC0, made quiet (`sounds/`): the click and the chime Kenney's, the keys recorded on six real keyboards, each key one of four strikes in turn. `defineFilm` takes `sounds: { keyboard }` (`membrane` where none is named, `mechanical`, `mechanical-bright`, `butterfly`, `laptop`, `clicky`), `sounds: { click, key, ending }`, files of the film's own in their place or `{ enabled: false }` for one turned off (`sounds: { key: { enabled: false } }`), or `sounds: { enabled: false }` for none, and `music: { file, volume }`, cut to the film's length and faded out at its end; `cursor.click(action, { sound })` gives one click its own file, and `sound.play(file)` plays a file at a moment of a step. The WebM carries the sound in Opus; a GIF holds none. A sound file that is not there, or that ffmpeg reads no sound from, is refused before the film is shot. ffmpeg needs an AAC encoder for the MP4, and Opus for a WebM.
- A camera that moves fast is blurred, as a film camera's open shutter blurs what moves: a frame is shot along the camera's way there from the frame before, the share of it the shutter is open for (all of it, unless the film says less), as many times as keep the copies a few pixels apart, eight at most, and the shots are blended; a smear shorter than that is shot once. `defineFilm` takes `motionBlur: { shutter, exposuresAtMost }`, either or both, the share of that way the shutter is open for and how many shots the fastest frame is blended from, or `motionBlur: { enabled: false }` for none. A still at such a moment is blended as the video's frame is; `--quality draft` shoots every frame once. A full shoot takes longer for it: the promo's took 549 s, against 183 s without, shot one after the other.
- `--renderer gpu` shoots a film or a still on the graphics card, in the same headless browser, which draws every frame in software otherwise, and needs no screen: the promo, wide and blurred, took 271 s against 495 s, shot one after the other, its frames alike. It reaches the card through Vulkan on Linux, through Metal on macOS and Direct3D 11 on Windows, the last two not tried yet; where it finds no driver for a graphics card, frames are drawn in software all the same, and on Linux, where that was tried, the page has no WebGL. `--renderer software` is the default: a film is shot as before.
- livereel's promo, `examples/livereel-promo.ts`, is shot square for a feed as well as wide, and heard. One script fits both frames: the camera leans back from the whole screen's pose, and the agent in its terminal shoots the format the promo itself is shot in, as an MP4 and a GIF. The README opens on it as a video with its sound, in place of a GIF.
- The package carries its source, `src/`, which its `exports` name under the `source` condition. 0.1.1, published by npm, named `./src/index.ts` there without carrying the file: its `publishConfig` was to take that name out, and npm does not apply `publishConfig`.
- A film shot at the finest quality is shot at twice the frame's width and height and scaled down, as it was meant to be: livereel captured frames on a CDP session of its own, which saw the page at its own pixels, so a full shoot was as soft as a draft, and a still the same to the byte. Frames are captured by Playwright now. A full shoot takes longer for the pixels: the promo took 151 s, and 52.6 s as a draft.

## 0.1.1

- A face-on camera too far back for the screen to fill the frame along a side sets the screen in the frame's middle along it: framing a wide block near the page's top showed the ground above the screen and none below.
- `terminal.open(title)` brings the terminal up over the frame along its share of a play, as `terminal.leave()` takes it away; in a cut it is there at once, as before.
- livereel's own promo, `examples/livereel-promo.ts`: an app served by the film itself, a real click on it, then the agent who made the film.
- The terminal's log no longer moves as the agent's work ends on a `work` or `done` line: every two lines of it stand as far apart, whatever their kinds.
- The terminal's line being typed is one line high again, its prompt at its start: it had kept the line breaks and the indent of the stage's markup.

## 0.1.0

- Film a live web page frame by frame: a camera with angles, the screen coming up, a light, a cursor, captions, a drawn agent's terminal and an ending card, on a clock of the film's own.
- A film is a TypeScript file whose default export is `defineFilm`; the package runs on Node 22.20 and later.
- `livereel shoot` makes the video as the film is shot, with a contact sheet (`--sheet`); `livereel still --at` shoots one still, refused at once past the film's end; `--draft` shoots either at the frame's own size; `livereel setup` installs the browser and looks for ffmpeg.
- A film of example.com, `examples/example-com.ts`.
- The `reel` skill: how an agent directs a film, installed with `npx skills add` or as the plugin.
